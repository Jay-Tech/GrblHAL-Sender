# JLCPCB assembly files

Generated by `python generate_jlcpcb.py` from the board and schematic. Regenerate rather than
hand-editing — these must stay consistent with each other and with the Gerbers.

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `bom.csv` | 13 distinct part types, grouped by (value, footprint) |
| `cpl.csv` | 43 SMD placements, rotations in **JLCPCB's convention** |

Gerbers are in `../ShopBoardV2OutPut/` (12 files) and `../ShopBoardV2OutPut.zip`.

## LCSC part numbers

12 of 13 lines carry a candidate C-code, filled from `LCSC` in `generate_jlcpcb.py`.

**These are candidates, not confirmations.** C-codes are stable identifiers, but stock and
Basic/Extended status are not — confirm every line in JLCPCB's own parts search. Basic parts
carry no per-part setup fee; Extended parts do, and at 13 unique types that is most of the
assembly cost on a small run.

### F1 has no LCSC candidate — decide this before ordering

The Bourns `MF-MSMF110/24X-2` does not appear in LCSC's catalogue. **Littelfuse `1812L110/24`
is the same specification** (1.1 A hold, 24 V, 1812) and is the first thing to search for.

**Do not accept a lower-voltage substitute.** No vendor makes a 1206 polyfuse above 16 V at
1.1 A hold, which is the entire reason this part is an 1812 — see
`../shop-output-board/design.md`. A 16 V part on a 24 V rail may fail rather than open at the
one moment it exists for.

If nothing suitable is stocked, the clean fallback is to **leave F1 unpopulated and hand-solder
it** — it is one part, and the pads are accessible.

### The optocoupler is rank C, and that is an upgrade

LCSC does not stock rank B. It stocks **A (C22113), C (C109227) and D (C114603)**.

The design needs **CTR ≥ 75%** — the opto must sink 3.36 mA through R2 against ~4.5 mA of LED
drive at 24 V. So:

| Rank | CTR | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A | 80–160% | **too close to the 75% floor**, especially as optos degrade with age |
| B | 130–260% | what the design specified |
| **C** | **200–400%** | **chosen — 2.7× margin at minimum rank** |
| D | 300–600% | also fine |

Higher CTR costs nothing here: R2 caps the collector current at 3.36 mA no matter how much
the opto *could* sink. So rank C is strictly better than the specified rank B. **Rank A is
the one to refuse.**

### Two substitutions worth a glance

- **Zener → `BZT52C8V2-7-F` (C500790).** Same 8.2 V, 500 mW, SOD-123 as the specified
  MMSZ5237B, and it is what LCSC actually stocks. Electrically interchangeable.
- **LED → C2297, a generic 0805 green.** The specified LTST-C170KGKT is high-efficiency for a
  reason: at a 5 V supply the indicator only draws **~1.4 mA**. Check the mcd rating before
  accepting, or the LEDs will be invisible at the bottom of the supply range. This is
  cosmetic, not functional — but the indicator existing at all is the point.

## Rotation: the thing that quietly ruins an assembled board

Part angles in `cpl.csv` are **not** KiCad's native ones. Three package families differ:

| Footprint | Correction applied | Parts |
|---|---|---|
| `Package_TO_SOT_SMD:SOT-23` | +180° | Q1–Q4, Q9 |
| `Package_SO:SOP-4_7.5x4.1mm_P2.54mm` | +270° | U1–U4 |
| `Capacitor_SMD:CP_Elec_6.3x7.7` | +180° | C1 |

Every one of those is polarized, so the wrong convention gives you a fully populated board of
backwards MOSFETs and optocouplers — and it passes every check you can run at home, because
nothing about the *board* is wrong.

These corrections were derived by diffing the KiCad Fabrication Toolkit's AUTO TRANSLATE
output against plain `kicad-cli pcb export pos`, on both v1 and v2. `generate_jlcpcb.py`
computes them independently and its output was checked against the plugin's: **all 43
placements agree.**

**Still look at JLCPCB's placement preview before paying.** It renders each part on the
footprint, and it is the last chance to catch a polarity error while it is free.

## Through-hole is excluded, deliberately

J1, J2, J4 and J5 — the two terminals and the two Pico sockets — appear in neither file. They
are hand-soldered. The BOM and CPL must agree on that, or the machine is told to place a part
it has no reel for.

## What has not happened

**Nothing in this design has been on a bench.** ERC, DRC, the netlist assertions and the pad
checks all confirm the board matches the design; none of them confirm the design works.
`../shop-output-board/bench-test.md` is the procedure, and the cost sequencing in
`../shop-output-board/fabrication.md` puts a breadboard and a bare-board run *before* paid
assembly — because assembled boards with a circuit fault are scrap, and the parts do not come
back off.
